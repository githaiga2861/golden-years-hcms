APPLICABILITY, SCOPE and PURPOSE of HOME HEALTH AGENCIES

WAC 246-335-300 through 246-335-355 / WAC 246-335-505 through WAC

246-335-560

WAC 246-335 Compliance

Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC acknowledges its review of WAC 246-335-300 through WAC 246-335-355, which outlines the general requirements for all in-home services agencies licensed in Washington State. We agree to follow these directives in all aspects of our operations.

Furthermore, Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC acknowledges its review of WAC 246-335-505 through WAC 246-335-560, which specifically applies to agencies licensed to provide home health services. We agree to follow these additional directives to ensure we meet the elevated standards of care required for home health services

Our Home Health Agency, Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC, implements programs and services to provide comprehensive, quality home health care in the patient’s home or service site. All services are provided under the direction of the Administrator and the Director of Clinical Services.

Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC addresses the patient's unique psychological, safety, self-esteem, and self-actualization needs in a patient plan of home health care.

Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC serves as a resource for the patient, the patient's family, and the community. We provide health care education for the patient, the patient's family and the community. Skilled nursing services will be provided by Registered Nurses and Licensed Practical Nurses (if hired by the Agency) and Home Health Aides. Physical, Occupational, Speech, Respiratory therapy, Dietitian/Nutritionist as well as Social Work services will be provided to the extent that such professionals are either hired by the Agency or contracted for by the Agency.

Similarly, services that may be provided by Physical, Occupational and Speech Therapists, and Medical Social Workers. Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC implements methods of screening applicants and evaluating their progress regularly in order to be able to provide quality personnel.

Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC provides staff developmental opportunities and programs, including orientation and programs concerning current practices in nursing and allied health care.

Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC provides current and accurate personnel records, including verification of current license/certification and health records.

The Agency maintains open communication with patients, employees, and Authorizing Practitioners via frequent contact in order to achieve quality and cost-effective care. The counties that we serve are {{mg_counties|Counties served|2.2in}}. Our hours of operation are {{mg_hours|Hours of operation|2.8in}}.

The Agency provides clinical services (either through its own staff or through contract) from Registered Nurses (“RNs”), Licensed Practical Nurses (“LPNs”), Home Health Aides (“HHAs”) Therapy services will be provided by Physical Therapists (“PTs”), Occupational Therapists (“OTs”), Speech Therapists (“SPs”), Respiratory Therapists (RT), Dietitian/Nutritionist and Medical Social Workers (“MSWs”) to the extent that the agency hires or contracts for such services. Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC adheres to standards set forth State, and Local regulations.

Applicability: This policy applies to all staff members, contractors, and volunteers of Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC, a skilled home care agency providing services in the state of Washington.

PURPOSE

This policy outlines the responsibilities of contractors and volunteers who provide services on behalf of the agency. It ensures they understand their roles, contribute to quality care, and comply with all applicable regulations as mandated by the state regulations.

Responsibilities

A. General Requirements

All contractors and volunteers will be required to:

1.

◦
Comply with Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC's non-discrimination policy as outlined in Chapter 49.60 RCW.
◦
Demonstrate qualifications relevant to their assigned tasks.
2.
Complete Application Process:
◦
Submit a formal application.
◦
Provide professional references.
◦
Undergo a background check in accordance with WAC 246-335-425(6).
3.
Sign Agreements:
◦
Sign a written agreement outlining:
▪
Specific services to be provided.
▪
Expected work hours.
▪
Commitment to adhering to agency policies and procedures, including confidentiality.
4.
Attend Orientation and Training:
◦
Participate in an orientation to Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC, including our mission, values, and client population.
◦
Receive training specific to their roles and responsibilities, ensuring competency in delivering safe and effective care.
◦
Complete ongoing training opportunities as required.
5.
Supervision:
◦
Work under the supervision of a qualified staff member, with the level of supervision dependent on the complexity of the service provided.
◦
Caregivers will always be directly supervised by the clinical manager.
6.

◦

Report any changes in client condition, concerns about client safety, or difficulties fulfilling assigned tasks.
8.
Compliance:
◦
Adhere to all applicable laws and regulations, including infection control protocols, HIPAA regulations, and Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC’s non-discrimination policy.

Maintain confidentiality of all client information.

◦
Background checks and disclosure statements will be renewed every two years.

Suitability Determinations:
◦
If a background check reveals non-disqualifying convictions, pending charges, or negative actions, Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC will conduct a character, competence, and suitability determination.

This determination will consider factors outlined in WAC 246-335-425(7) to assess the individual's ability to provide safe and appropriate care.

ACCIDENT PREVENTION

If you are over age 65, your chances of dying from an accident are almost twice as likely as that of any other age group. By taking the right precautions, you can protect yourself and those around you and prevent serious injury.

Why is injury a common problem among older people?

1.
During the aging process certain physical, mental and emotional changes occur:
2.
Less physical strength
3.
Impaired eyesight
4.
Impaired hearing
5.
Slower physical reaction
6.
Poor balance and coordination

What was considered a “minor” accident in your younger years may be serious

now due to

1.
Lower resistance to disease
2.
Slower healing
3.
Bones that are brittle
4.
Your particular illness or disease
5.
Physical limitations

The most common and dangerous cause of injury is falls.

Here are measures you can take to reduce falls

1.
Remove scatter rugs or use non-skid tape or backing on throw rugs
2.
Tack down the edges of all carpets
3.
Never leave articles around beds, stairs, or in hallways
4.
Do not use a doorway, halls or stairs for storage
5.
Keep pathways clear of furniture, electric cords, space heaters, etc.
6.
Don’t rush when climbing up or down stairs
7.
Stairs should have non-skid treads and a solid, easy to grasp handrail
8.
If you must climb, use a solid step or ladder rather than a chair or box
9.
When carrying objects, make sure you:
a.
Can see
b.
Get a firm grip
c.
Move slowly and evenly
d.
Lift with your legs (knees bent, back straight)
e.
Ask for help with heavy or awkward objects

General Safety Tips

1.
Avoid wearing only socks, smooth-soled shoes or slippers on non-carpeted floors
2.
Avoid wet floors – wipe all spills up immediately
3.
Keep kitchen floor free of grease and scraps
4.
Household pets should be kept under control and out of pathways
5.
To avoid dizziness, get out of your bed or chair slowly.
6.
In the bathroom:
a.
Be sure mats are non-skid and there are treads in the tub or shower to prevent slips.
b.
Install “grab bars”. Towel racks should not be used as grab bars as they are not secure enough to support body weight.

Adequate lighting will help prevent accidents

1.
Keep a lamp near the bed so you will not have to get up in the dark.
2.
Keep a night light in the bathroom.
3.
Keep hallways and steps well-lit.
4.
Keep a flashlight handy in case of power failure

Post Emergency Numbers By Your Phone
2.

3.
Take your time

Hazard Vulnerability Assessment (HVA) for a Home Health Agency

Introduction

A Hazard Vulnerability Assessment (HVA) is a critical tool for home health agencies to identify and evaluate potential risks that could impact their operations and the safety of their clients and staff. By conducting a thorough HVA, agencies can develop effective mitigation strategies to minimize the impact of hazards and ensure business continuity.

Steps

1.
Identify Hazards:
◦
Natural Hazards: Earthquakes, floods, hurricanes, wildfires, tornadoes, winter storms, etc.
◦
Man-Made Hazards: Power outages, chemical spills, terrorism, active shooter incidents, etc.
◦
Operational Hazards: Equipment failures, data breaches, staffing shortages, etc.
2.
Assess Vulnerability:
◦
Evaluate the agency's susceptibility to each identified hazard. Consider factors such as location, infrastructure, resources, and emergency preparedness plans.
◦
Determine the potential impact of each hazard on the agency's operations, clients, and staff.
3.
Prioritize Risks:
◦
Rank the identified hazards based on their likelihood of occurrence and potential severity.
◦
Focus on mitigating risks that pose the greatest threat to the agency's mission and continuity of operations.
4.
Develop Mitigation Strategies:
◦
Create strategies to reduce the likelihood of hazards occurring or to minimize their impact.
◦
Consider measures such as emergency preparedness plans, evacuation procedures, backup systems, and staff training.
5.
Implement Mitigation Strategies:
◦
Ensure that mitigation strategies are implemented effectively and regularly tested.
◦
Document all actions taken to address identified risks.
6.
Monitor and Review:
◦
Regularly review and update the HVA to reflect changes in the agency's operations, environment, and regulatory requirements.
◦
Continuously monitor for new or emerging hazards and adjust mitigation strategies accordingly.

Key Areas for Assessment

•
Physical Infrastructure: Assess the vulnerability of buildings, equipment, and infrastructure to natural and man-made hazards.
•
Staff Safety: Evaluate the agency's ability to protect staff from hazards and ensure their well-being.
•
Client Safety: Assess the potential risks to clients, including those with mobility limitations or health conditions.
•
Operational Continuity: Evaluate the agency's ability to maintain essential services during and after a disaster.
•
Data Security: Assess the risk of data breaches and implement measures to protect sensitive information.

•

•

•

Any allergies or contraindications documented in the patient's medical record.

Expiry date of the medication.

•
Patient Education: Nurses will educate patients and their caregivers about their medications, including expected benefits, potential side effects, and proper storage.
•
Reporting: Any adverse medication reactions or medication errors will be reported immediately to the supervising physician or practitioner and documented in the patient's medical record.

Treatment Orders: Home health staff will only provide treatments as ordered by a licensed physician or practitioner.

Staff members of the Agency will not engage in the pronouncement of death. Agency Registered Nurse (RN) will contact the M.D. for appropriate instructions that may include notifying civil authorities or 911.

In the event of an expected death, the Physician, and Funeral Director, should be notified.
2.

a.
ADNR/Advance Directive should be established
b.
The DNR/Advance Directive should be registered with the medical examiner.
3.
The nurse will remain with the family until the funeral home staff has removed the body.
4.
In the event of an unexpected death, the County Coroner, police, physician, and the Agency office are to be contacted. 5.

6.

7.
All actions, interventions, and observations are documented in the clinical record.
8.
Any controlled substances remaining are to be destroyed by the family, if possible, prior to the nurse leaving the home. No controlled substances are to be removed from the home. The nurse is to notify the police if the family does not destroy the medication.

b.

c.

d.

e.
If the emergency medical system is activated:
i.
The EMS personnel, or physician, may decide to transport the patient.
ii.
The EMS personnel may terminate measures, in the home, under physician orders.
10.
Educating the patient, or family, concerning the death process, and what to expect, will help lessen family stress.
11.
Notify the Director of Nursing or designee, of patient’s death.
12.
Notify the primary physician of patient’s death.

•
Back-up care for patient ADLs (Activities of Daily Living): This includes assistance with tasks such as bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, and ambulation.
•
Back-up care for patient health services: This includes assistance with tasks requiring minimum healthcare credentialing, such as medication administration, wound care, or monitoring vital signs.

Non credentialed staff may provide back-up care only when assisting a patient with ADLs or in emergency situations

Staff Qualifications for Back-up Care

•
Back-up care for patient ADLs: Non-credentialed staff, such as trained personal care assistants, may provide back-up care when assisting a patient with ADLs. These staff members will receive training specific to the needs of the patient and demonstrate competency in performing ADL tasks safely and effectively.
•
Back-up care for patient health services: Back-up care requiring assistance with patient health services must be provided by staff with the minimum healthcare credentialing necessary for the specific task. This may include licensed nurses, therapists, or certified nursing assistants (CNAs) depending on the service required.

Scheduling Back-up Care

•
The agency will make every effort to ensure scheduled caregivers are available to provide services.
•
In the event a scheduled caregiver is unavailable due to illness, vacation, or other unforeseen circumstances, the agency will take the following steps:
◦
Notify the patient or their designated representative as soon as possible.
◦
Assess the patient's needs and determine the appropriate type of back-up care required.
◦
Identify qualified back-up staff based on the patient's needs and credentialing requirements.
◦
Communicate the back-up care plan to the patient, family, and back-up staff member.

Emergency Situations

•
In emergency situations where a qualified back-up staff member is unavailable, the agency may temporarily assign non-credentialed staff to assist with basic patient needs, such as ensuring the patient's safety and comfort, until qualified medical personnel arrive.
•
Non-credentialed staff will be instructed to avoid performing any tasks that require healthcare training and to document the situation accurately.

Communication and Documentation

•
All back-up care occurrences will be documented in the patient's medical record, including the reason for the need for back-up care, the type of care provided, and the qualifications of the back-up staff member.
•
The agency will communicate any back-up care incidents to the patient's physician, particularly if non-credentialed staff were involved in an emergency situation.

NURSE DELEGATION OF TASKS

RCW 18.79.260

Statement of Purpose

This policy outlines the guidelines for registered nurses (RNs) to safely delegate specific nursing tasks to other qualified healthcare personnel. It ensures patient safety by establishing competency requirements, delegation appropriateness criteria, and supervision protocols.

Statement

1.
RN Delegation Authority: RNs can delegate tasks to qualified individuals if deemed best for the patient's care.
(a)
Delegation is only permitted for stable and predictable patients requiring specific nursing tasks that do not require clinical judgment
(b)
An approved home health service category only may use their RN on staff for patient nurse delegation needs
(c)
Approved home health and home care service categories may provide nurse delegation in the following ways:
▪
Use an RN from their home health side to contract with and delegate to their home care side
▪
Transfer a home care client needing delegation to the agency's home health side
2.
RN Responsibilities During Delegation:
◦
Competency Assessment: Ensure the individual possesses the skills to perform the delegated tasks.
◦
Delegation Appropriateness: Evaluate if delegating the task aligns with the patient's condition and the individual's competency.
◦
Supervision: Provide ongoing oversight and guidance to the individual performing the delegated task.
◦
Scope of Practice: Delegate tasks solely within the RN's scope of practice.
3.
Restrictions on Delegation:
◦
Administration of medications (except for specific exceptions).
◦
Tasks requiring substantial skill.
◦
Piercing or severing tissues.
◦
Tasks requiring nursing judgment.
4.
Protection from Employer Reprisal:
◦
RNs cannot be pressured to delegate if they believe it compromises patient safety.
◦
They are immune from employer discipline for refusing delegation due to safety concerns.
5.
Delegation in Community-Based/In-Home Care:
◦
Delegation is only allowed for registered/certified nursing assistants or certified home care aides (if they have an NA-C or NA-R license)
◦
Simple tasks (e.g., blood pressure monitoring) are exempt from this requirement.
◦
Applicable only to patients with stable and predictable conditions.
◦
RNs retain discretion to determine delegation appropriateness.
◦
Insulin injections, sterile procedures, and central line maintenance are never delegable.
6.
Delegation of Insulin Injections:
◦
RNs can delegate insulin injections only after:
▪
Instructing the individual on proper injection procedures and insulin use.
▪
Demonstrating proper injection techniques.
▪
Supervising and evaluating the individual's performance as per commission rules.
◦
Supervision and evaluation frequency are determined by the commission.
7.
RN Accountability:
◦
The RN is accountable for their actions throughout the delegation process.
◦
Nurses acting within delegation protocols are immune from liability for actions performed during delegation duties.
8.
Purpose of Delegation Protocols:
◦
To ensure consistent standards of nursing practice.
◦
To safeguard the RN's authority for independent delegation decisions.
9.
Additional Delegation: Delegation can include glucose monitoring and testing to qualified personnel.
10.
Rule-Making Authority: The Nursing Care Quality Assurance Commission holds the authority to adopt rules for implementing this policy.
11.
Instruction by RNs:

Only licensed RNs can instruct nurses on technical nursing subjects.

12.
Public Representation:

Only licensed RNs can identify themselves as registered nurses to the public. An approved home health service category only may use their RN on staff for patient nurse delegation needs

13.
DSHS training: (14)(d) Home health aides must complete the DSHS nurse delegation class prior to participating in the delegation process. If the tasks are ones considered by the nursing quality assurance commission to be simple care tasks, such as blood pressure monitoring, personal care services, diabetic insulin device set up, and verbal verification of insulin dosage for sight-impaired individuals, the DSHS training is not required.

Ensure the safety and well-being of vulnerable clients receiving care from the home health agency.

◦

◦
(e) Time Elapsed: Consider the number of years that have passed since the offense, negative action, or other issue.
◦
(f) Self-Disclosure: Consider whether the individual self-disclosed the convictions, pending charges, or negative actions.
◦
(g) Health and Safety Concerns: Evaluate any other health and safety concerns that may impact client care.
◦
(h) Department Authority: Acknowledge that although the agency may determine suitability, the Washington State Department of Health (DOH) has the final authority regarding professional credentials.

Hand Hygiene Policy

Purpose

To establish uniform procedures for effective hand hygiene practices among all Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC personnel, contractors, and volunteers. Proper hand hygiene minimizes the risk of transmitting infections to clients, staff, and visitors during the delivery of in-home health services.

Policy

All employees, contractors, and volunteers of Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC must comply with current Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and Washington State Department of Health hand hygiene guidelines. Hand hygiene must be performed before and after any direct client contact and at other key moments outlined in this policy.

Failure to adhere to this policy may result in disciplinary action and retraining requirements.

Procedure

A.
Indications for Handwashing

Employees shall wash hands with soap and running water in the following circumstances:

1.
When hands are visibly soiled or contaminated with blood, body fluids, or other material.
2.
Before eating and after using the restroom.
3.
Before and after direct client contact.
4.
Before inserting urinary catheters, IV lines, or any invasive devices.
5.
After contact with body fluids, mucous membranes, or non-intact skin.
6.
After removing gloves.
7.
When moving from a contaminated body site to a clean body site during client care.
8.
After contact with environmental surfaces or equipment near the client.
B.
Indications for Alcohol-Based Hand Rub

When hands are not visibly soiled, an alcohol-based hand rub (containing 60–95% ethanol or isopropanol) may be used in place of soap and water:

1.
Before and after direct client contact.
2.
Before handling medication or clean supplies.
3.
After removing gloves.
4.
After contact with intact skin, such as taking a pulse or blood pressure.
5.
After touching equipment or other objects in the client’s environment.
C.
Handwashing Technique
1.
Wet hands under clean, running water.
2.
Apply antimicrobial or non-antimicrobial soap.

TUBERCULOSIS INFECTION CONTROL PROGRAM

Tuberculosis (TB) is a serious infectious disease that can affect the lungs. This program outlines the procedures for preventing the transmission of TB within Golden Years Home Health Supported Living LLC, a home health skilled agency, to protect our employees, patients, and their families, in accordance with Washington Administrative Code (WAC) 246-335-525(15)(c). Risk Assessment

•
New Employees: All new employees will undergo a TB risk assessment upon hire, following the requirements of WAC 246-335-525(15)(c). This assessment will be conducted using the form provided by the Department of Health (DOH). It will consider factors such as:
◦
Previous TB diagnosis or exposure
◦
Immigration history from high-burden TB countries
◦
Work experience in healthcare settings
◦
Presence of risk factors for latent TB infection (LTBI)
•
Testing Based on Risk Assessment: Based on the DOH-provided risk assessment results, the agency will determine the need for TB testing of new employees:
◦
Employees identified as high-risk will undergo testing for LTBI using a Mantoux tuberculin skin test (TST) or interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA) blood test, following the DOH's tuberculosis risk assessment form testing recommendations.
◦
Employees with a positive TB test will be referred to a healthcare provider for further evaluation and treatment.
◦
Employees with negative test results will be documented and monitored according to their risk category.
•
Annual Risk Assessments: All employees will undergo annual assessments for new TB risk factors using the DOH-provided form. This ensures timely identification of individuals who may require further evaluation or testing.

Training and Education

•
In compliance with WAC 246-335-525(15)(c)(iii), all new employees will receive TB-related training and education at the time of hire or during new employee orientation. This training will be consistent with the DOH's tuberculosis program's online posted educational materials and will cover:
◦
Understanding TB transmission and symptoms
◦
Importance of TB risk assessments and testing
◦
Proper use of personal protective equipment (PPE) when indicated
◦
Infection control practices for preventing the spread of TB
◦
Procedures for reporting suspected TB cases to the appropriate authorities

Actions When Infectious Disease Symptoms Are Present

•
Adhering to WAC 246-100 and 246-101 WAC, the agency will have established procedures for responding to situations where personnel, volunteers, contractors, or clients exhibit or report symptoms of a communicable disease in an infectious stage. These procedures will include:
◦
Prompt identification and assessment of symptoms by a qualified healthcare professional.
◦
Implementation of appropriate infection control measures to prevent the spread of the disease, following guidance from chapters 246-100 and 246-101 WAC.
◦
Exclusion of individuals with suspected or confirmed contagious diseases from direct patient care activities until they are no longer infectious, as determined by a healthcare professional.
◦
Reporting of confirmed cases of communicable diseases to the local health department, as required by law.
◦
Communication with affected individuals and their families regarding the situation and necessary precautions.

Additional Considerations

•
The agency will maintain a record of all TB risk assessments, testing results, and employee education completion.
•
N95 respirators or other appropriate PPE will be readily available for use in situations where exposure to a suspected TB case is possible.
•
The agency will develop a communication plan to inform staff about suspected or confirmed TB cases while protecting patient confidentiality.
•
The agency will collaborate with the DOH for guidance, resources, and reporting requirements related to TB infection control.

Program Review

This TB infection control program will be reviewed and updated annually to reflect any changes in DOH recommendations or agency practices.

WAC 246-335-540

Policy

This document outlines the process for developing and implementing a written home health plan of care (POC) for patients receiving services from our Washington State home health agency. It emphasizes collaboration with the patient, their designated family member/legal representative, and the authorizing practitioner.

Process

1.
Initial Assessment:
a.
A registered nurse (RN) or other qualified healthcare professional will conduct a comprehensive assessment of the patient's needs upon referral.
b.
This assessment will involve:
i.
Reviewing the patient's medical history and current condition.
ii.
Assessing the patient's functional abilities and limitations in activities of daily living (ADLs) like bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, and mobility.
iii.
Identifying the patient's psychosocial needs and social support system.
iv.
Considering the patient's home environment and safety hazards.
2.
Gathering Input:
a.
The RN will involve the patient, their designated family member/legal representative (if applicable), and the authorizing practitioner in developing the POC.
b.
This can be achieved through:
i.
Patient interviews
ii.
Family/representative meetings
iii.
Communication with the authorizing practitioner (physician) through phone calls, chart reviews, or progress notes.
3.
Developing the POC:
a.
The RN will draft the POC based on the assessment findings and gathered input.
b.
The POC will address the following elements:
i.
Patient Information: Name, date of birth, contact information.
ii.
Diagnosis: Primary medical condition(s) prompting home health services.
iii.
Goals: Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) goals for improvement in ADLs, wound healing, pain management, etc.
iv.
Interventions: The specific home health services to be provided (e.g., skilled nursing care, physical therapy, occupational therapy, medication management, personal care assistance).
v.
Frequency and Duration: How often and for how long each service will be provided.
vi.
Teaching and Training: Specific skills or self-care activities the patient or caregiver will be trained on.
vii.
Plan of Care Review and Revision Schedule: Frequency for reviewing and updating the POC based on patient progress.
viii.
Disciplinary Standing Orders: Standing orders from the authorizing practitioner for routine medications and procedures.
ix.
Discharge Planning: Strategies for transitioning the patient back to the community or to a higher level of care if needed.
x.
Palliative care if applicable
4.
Communication and Collaboration:
a.
The draft POC will be shared with the patient, family/representative, and authorizing practitioner for review and agreement.
b.
Any questions or concerns will be addressed collaboratively.
c.
Once finalized, all parties will receive a copy of the signed POC.
5.
Implementing the POC:
a.
The home health agency staff will implement the POC according to the outlined interventions, frequency, and duration under the supervision of the administrator.
b.
Documentation will be maintained in the patient's medical record for all services provided.
c.
Communication channels will be established for the patient and family/representative to report any changes in condition or concerns.
6.
Review and Revision:
a.
The POC will be reviewed at regular intervals (as defined in the plan) or more frequently if necessary based on patient progress.
b.
The review will involve the patient, family/representative, and authorizing practitioner.
c.
The POC will be revised as needed to reflect changes in the patient's condition, treatment plan, or goals.
7.
Each patient will have a written home health plan of care developed and implemented with input from the patient, designated family member, or legal representative and authorizing practitioner.
8.
"Authorizing practitioner" means the individual practitioners licensed in Washington state, or another state according to the exemption criteria established in chapters 18.57, 18.71, and 18.79 RCW, and authorized to approve a home health plan of care:

A podiatric physician and surgeon licensed under chapter 18.22 RCW; or

An advanced registered nurse practitioner (ARNP), as authorized under chapter 18.79 RCW.

WAC 246-335-555 QUALITY IMPROVEMENT PROGRAM

This policy outlines the implementation of a quality improvement program (QIP) throughout all our approved home health service areas. This program ensures we continuously monitor, evaluate, and improve the quality of care and services we provide, adhering to WAC 246-335-555.

1.
QIP Structure

Our QIP will consist of the following core elements:

•
Quality Improvement Committee: A designated committee comprised of qualified healthcare professionals, staff representatives, and, when appropriate, consumer representatives, will oversee the QIP.
•
Performance Measures: We will identify key performance indicators (KPIs) aligned with national and state quality standards for home health care. These KPIs may include:
◦
Client satisfaction with care
◦
Readmission rates to hospitals
◦
Rates of hospital-acquired infections
◦
Medication adherence rates
◦
Pain management effectiveness
◦
Client functional outcomes
•
Data Collection and Analysis: We will establish a system for collecting data on our identified KPIs. Data collection methods may involve client surveys, chart reviews, incident reports, and staff feedback. The QIP committee will regularly analyze this data to identify trends and areas for improvement.
•
Quality Improvement Activities: Based on data analysis, the QIP committee will recommend and implement quality improvement activities. These activities may involve:
◦
Updating policies and procedures.
◦
Implementing new training programs for staff.
◦
Piloting new care models or interventions.
◦
Revising plans of care to address identified needs.
•
Communication and Reporting: The QIP committee will communicate program findings and improvement activities to all staff and leadership. Regular reports on the QIP will be submitted to the appropriate regulatory agencies.

Service Area Integration: KPIs and data collection methods will be adapted to address the specific needs and services provided in each area.

Communication and Collaboration: Staff from all service areas will be encouraged to participate in the QIP process by sharing best practices and feedback.
•
Benchmarking: We will benchmark our performance against national and state quality standards, as well as compare data across service areas to identify areas for improvement.

The QIP is an ongoing process committed to continuous improvement in the quality of care provided by our agency. The program will be reviewed and updated regularly to reflect changes in regulations, best practices, and client needs.

Information on any corrective actions taken

Medical Supplies and Equipment Management

5.
Inventory and Maintenance:
◦
A comprehensive inventory of all medical supplies and equipment will be maintained.
◦
A schedule for routine cleaning, inspection, repair, and calibration of equipment will be established based on manufacturer's recommendations and industry standards.
◦
Records will be maintained documenting the date, type of maintenance performed, and the name of the individual who performed the maintenance.
6.
Safe Handling and Storage:
◦
Medical supplies and equipment will be stored in a clean, dry, and secure location, following manufacturer's recommendations for temperature, humidity, and light exposure.
◦
Expired or damaged supplies and equipment will be promptly removed from use and disposed of according to appropriate regulations.
7.
Patient Education and Consent:
◦
For patients requiring durable medical equipment (DME) or other medical supplies at home, the agency will:
▪
Inform the patient, designated family member, or legal representative about the cost and payment options for the equipment, repairs, and replacements.
▪
Obtain documented approval from the patient or authorized representative before acquiring the equipment.
▪
Provide clear and comprehensive instructions on the proper use and maintenance of the equipment in a format understandable to the patient or family member. This may include written instructions, verbal instructions, or demonstrations.
▪
Document the patient or family member's understanding of the instructions provided.
8.
Equipment Replacement:
◦
Supplies and equipment essential for patient health or safety will be replaced as needed. This includes:
▪
Proactive replacement based on established schedules and expiration dates.
▪
Reactive replacement of malfunctioning or damaged equipment.
▪
Replacing equipment recalled by the manufacturer to ensure patient safety.

◦

◦

◦

the patient, designated family member, or legal representative's approval

Training

•
Staff will receive ongoing training on this policy, proper use of medical supplies and equipment, and safe handling procedures.

Contractor Selection and Oversight

•
During the contractor selection process, the agency will evaluate the vendor's policies and procedures for managing medical supplies and equipment.
•
The agency will only contract with vendors who demonstrate practices consistent with WAC 246-841-001(1), as outlined in this policy.
•
Contractual Requirements:
◦
The contract with the vendor will explicitly reference this policy and require the vendor to comply with all its provisions.
◦
The contract will outline the agency's right to audit the vendor's practices related to medical supplies and equipment management.

Monitoring and Enforcement

•
The agency will conduct periodic audits or reviews to ensure the contracted service provider adheres to the agreed-upon policies and procedures for medical supplies and equipment management.
•
This may involve reviewing the vendor's documentation for maintenance logs, inventory records, and staff training related to medical supplies and equipment use.
•
If any discrepancies or non-compliance issues are identified, the agency will take corrective action, which may include:
◦
Issuing a corrective action plan for the vendor to address deficiencies.
◦
Withholding payment until corrective actions are implemented.
◦
Termination of the contract in cases of severe or persistent non-compliance.

If the Agency contracts for medical supplies or equipment services

7.
Contractor Selection
•
Prior to contracting with a medical supply and equipment service provider, a thorough evaluation process will be conducted. This will assess the contractor's:
◦
Licenses and certifications in Washington State.
◦
Experience and reputation in providing medical supplies and equipment.
◦
Policies and procedures related to infection control, patient safety, and quality assurance.
◦
References from other healthcare agencies.
•
The agency will only contract with vendors who demonstrate adherence to high standards for quality, safety, and infection control practices.
8.
Contractual Requirements
•
All contracts with medical supply and equipment service providers will include clear language outlining the following:
◦
The specific medical supplies and equipment to be provided.
◦
Delivery schedules and procedures.
◦
Maintenance and repair protocols for equipment.
◦
Infection control practices for cleaning, disinfecting, and storing equipment.
◦
Procedures for handling product recalls and defects.
◦
A communication plan for addressing any concerns or issues regarding supplies or equipment.
◦
Confidentiality requirements for protecting patient information.
9.
Contractor Monitoring
•
The agency will establish a system for monitoring the performance of our contracted medical supply and equipment service providers. This may include:
◦
Regular reviews of contractor policies and procedures.
◦
Site visits to observe the contractor's operations.
◦
Reviewing customer satisfaction surveys or feedback reports.
◦
Monitoring for recalls or safety alerts related to medical supplies or equipment provided by the contractor.
10.
Corrective Action
•
If the agency identifies any concerns regarding a contractor's compliance with infection control standards, patient safety protocols, or contractual obligations, a corrective action plan will be implemented.
•
This plan may involve requiring the contractor to revise their procedures, conduct additional training for their staff, or potentially terminate the contract if serious issues persist.
11.
Documentation
•
The agency will maintain complete records of all contracts with medical supply and equipment service providers, including:
◦
Evaluation documents used in selecting the contractor.
◦
The signed contract outlining terms and expectations.
◦
Documentation of any monitoring activities or communication with the contractor.
◦
Records of any corrective action plans implemented.
12.
Compliance
•
This policy is designed to ensure our agency adheres to subsection (1) of this section and all applicable Washington State regulations related to infection control, patient safety, and contracting for medical supplies and equipment services.

By implementing this policy, our home health agency can foster strong working relationships with reliable and compliant medical supply and equipment service providers. This ultimately benefits patient care by ensuring access to safe and effective medical supplies and equipment.
